01Why think in stages rather than tools?

Every lead for a plumber, roofer or law firm is a person moving through the same four steps, whatever the trade. First, the person searches and finds you. Second, the person calls, texts or fills in a form. Third, someone answers and books the job, or the caller tries the next business on the list. Finally, the customer needs a reminder, a nudge on an estimate, or a reason to come back next season.

An AI tool only adds leads if the tool fixes the step where you are losing leads. For example, a beautiful new website does nothing if calls go to voicemail after 5pm. A 24/7 AI receptionist does little if nobody can find you on Google Maps. So before buying anything, look at where the gap is:

  • Few calls and enquiries? Start with getting found.
  • Plenty of calls, but missed or slow callbacks? Start with answering.
  • Estimates that go quiet and customers who never return? Start with follow-up.
  • You sell to other businesses and need more of them? Consider outbound.

02Get found: Google Business Profile, website and reviews tools

For many local service businesses, the Google Business Profile is a major source of calls. According to Google, local results are based on three factors: relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well a profile matches the search, and distance is how far the business is from the person searching. Prominence is how well-known a business is, based partly on links and reviews, and Google says more reviews and positive ratings can help a business’s local ranking. For example, a roofer with a complete profile, accurate service areas and a steady flow of new reviews gives Google more to work with than a roofer with an empty profile. Distance is the one factor no tool can change, so the first job is relevance and prominence. The tools here fall into three groups: software you run yourself, done-for-you services, and features to skip because Google retired the features in 2024 and 2025.

Google Business Profile and review software

Software you run yourself to schedule posts, track map rankings and collect reviews. Well-known examples include BrightLocal, Localo and Merchynt's Paige, which uses AI to post to and optimize the profile. Podium and Birdeye focus on review requests by text and on messaging. AI features in these tools draft posts and review replies; you still decide what goes out.

04Follow up: reminders, estimate chasing and reactivation

Follow-up tools do not create new leads, but they turn more of the ones you have into jobs. Three jobs matter most: confirming and reminding booked appointments so fewer people no-show, chasing estimates that went quiet, and bringing past customers back for the next service or season.

Start with what you already own. Field service software such as ServiceTitan, Jobber and Housecall Pro is where your jobs and customer history live, so check which reminders and follow-up messages it can already send. Email marketing tools such as Mailchimp handle seasonal campaigns to past customers, and review platforms such as Podium and Birdeye send review requests by text.

Keres AI sells two workers for this stage, each quoted to your volume:

  • Theo sends appointment confirmations and reminders and follows up on open estimates.
  • June runs reactivation campaigns to past customers and asks for reviews after jobs.

The test for any follow-up tool is simple: does it work from your real customer and job list, and does it stop when the customer books, replies or asks it to?

05Outbound: when cold email fits, and when it does not

Outbound email tools find prospects and send personalized sequences. Well-known examples include Apollo, Instantly and Smartlead. Keres AI's Sol writes and sends outbound email for you, quoted to your volume.

Be honest about fit. Cold email rarely works for homeowner services. Nobody books an emergency plumber or a roof repair from an unsolicited email, and homeowners searching for help are on Google, not in their inbox. For those businesses, money spent on getting found and answering will usually return more.

Outbound does fit local businesses that sell to other businesses: commercial cleaning, commercial HVAC maintenance contracts, property management services, landscaping for offices and HOAs, and firms that win work through referral partners. If your best customers are businesses with a decision-maker you can name, outbound is worth testing. If they are households, skip it.

06In what order should you add AI tools?

For most local service businesses, the best order is the one that gets the most leads for the least effort:

  1. Fix answering first. If calls are being missed, every other tool sends more leads into the same leak. An AI receptionist or missed-call text-back is usually the fastest win, for example Remi by Keres AI or a text-back feature in your phone system.
  2. Then get found. A current Google Business Profile, steady reviews and a website that loads fast on a phone and makes calling easy.
  3. Then follow up. Reminders, estimate chasing and past-customer campaigns, starting with what ServiceTitan, Jobber or Housecall Pro already does.
  4. Outbound last, and only if you sell to businesses.
